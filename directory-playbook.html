Is ChatGPT naming other tools and not yours? Here's how we got listed in one day, for $0

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · July 25, 2026

The short version: ChatGPT named three tools for a question our customers ask, and ours wasn't one. My take: AI search tools build answers from pages like directories, roundups, and alternatives lists, so we spent one day getting our product onto as many of those as we could reach. Four directories submitted, two roundup authors emailed, one listing verified the same day. Total spend: $0. We turned down every paid upsell along the way. Here's the exact route, tolls and all.

Why we did this (the test that started it)

We asked ChatGPT a question our own customers might ask: "check ai visibility." It answered confidently. It defined the term, offered help, and named three free tools. Ours wasn't one of them. Next to two giants sat a tool almost nobody has heard of. When we looked into how a no-name tool got that spot, we found it on the kinds of pages ChatGPT's search reads: directories, comparison articles, and alternatives lists. My guess is that's why, but we can't prove it. That fits outside research too: more than 87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top 20 results in one study (Seer Interactive, Feb 2025). My take: the list isn't magic. It's built from pages, and you can get yourself onto them.

The patterns nobody warns you about

1. The free tier was always there, and usually buried

Every single directory had a $0 path. Only one made it the obvious choice. We saw a pre-selected $288 annual plan above a small "launch free" link, a "featured" pitch calibrated by a quiz about our goals, and a paid tier whose main benefit was skipping a queue we were in no hurry to skip. If you submit your business anywhere: scroll past the shiny button and find the free door. It's there.

2. Some tolls are actually good deals

SaaSHub requires you to name competitors. That felt like homework until we realized it places your product on those competitors' "alternatives" pages. We named the giants in our space and earned twelve placements beside them for free. Read every requirement twice; some are gifts wearing chores' clothing.

3. The badge toll is a real decision

One directory wouldn't unlock our free listing until its badge (a cartoon mascot) was live on our homepage. Their crawler checked. We chose to pay it (small, low-opacity, in the footer) and calendared a review date to measure whether the listing sends enough visitors to justify it. Their growth loop, our call to keep or cut. If a badge-for-listing trade ever feels wrong for your brand, it's allowed to be a no.

What this means if you run a local business, not a software tool

Everything above is the same game your business is already in. Only the shelf changes. My take: for a barbershop, a winery, or a contractor, the listings that matter most are Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Yelp, and Apple Business, plus your chamber of commerce. Complete, accurate listings that all agree are your version of this playbook. They put your real details on the pages these tools can find before anyone asks about you.

Honest limit: none of this guarantees a recommendation. Listings take days to weeks to go live, engines re-crawl on their own schedule, and nobody can sell you a guaranteed mention in an AI's answer. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling weather. What this work does is make you present in the sources. Presence is the ticket; the engines still pick the winners.

FAQ

Why do directories matter for being recommended by ChatGPT?

When ChatGPT's search answers a question like 'what are the best free AI visibility tools,' it doesn't use a secret internal list. It reads pages from the open web. In one study, more than 87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top 20 results for the same question (Seer Interactive, Feb 2025). My take: directories, comparison roundups, and alternatives pages are the kind of pages that show up for 'best tools' questions, so a listing there gives you a shot at being in what it reads. It doesn't guarantee a mention.

Do you have to pay to get listed in AI tool directories?

Usually no, but most directories we tried put paid upsells ahead of the free path. In one day we submitted to four directories for $0 total and turned down every upgrade offered: a $75 priority queue, a $24/month featured membership pre-selected at $288/year billing, and $149-$199 one-time promotional boosts. Free tiers exist; they are just rarely the big shiny button.

What is a verification badge requirement?

Some directories require you to embed their badge on your website before your free listing goes live. Their crawler checks your homepage for the badge link. It's the toll for the free tier: you get a listing, they get a backlink from every listed site. Decide deliberately whether the placement is worth a third-party badge on your site, and where to put it (a small footer placement costs the least).

How does this apply to a local business, not a software tool?

My take: the idea is the same; only the shelf changes. For a local business, the listings that matter most are Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Yelp, Apple Business, and your chamber of commerce. Google says a page has to be indexed in Google Search to show up as a link in its AI answers, and in one study most ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top results, so being complete, accurate, and consistent in those places is the same kind of work. It puts your real details where these tools can find them. It doesn't guarantee anyone names you.
