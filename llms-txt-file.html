The pattern, if you're writing your own

The file above isn't generated by a tool, and it isn't following some strict spec. It's plain text, shaped the way most working examples are shaped: a heading with the site's name, a quick pitch, a short summary, then a couple of link sections. Each link gets a one-line description.

Nobody enforces that shape, which is also why llms.txt files you find online rarely look identical to each other. There's no single correct format — just a common one people converged on.

How to check if you actually have one

Type yoursite.com/llms.txt into your browser. A 404 means you don't have one — that's the entire check. Or skip typing URLs and run a free AO Audit scan, which looks for llms.txt automatically alongside schema markup and AI-crawler access.

How to make your own

Open a plain text editor. Add a heading with your business name, a short paragraph on what you do and where, then link your most important pages — homepage, pricing, anything with real facts about the business. Save it as llms.txt, drop it in your site's root folder next to robots.txt, and you're done. No generator, no plugin. Fifteen minutes for most small businesses, not a project.

One caveat worth saying plainly: llms.txt is young. Nobody enforces the format, and there's no promise every AI system reads it yet. Still, it takes minutes to add and nothing to skip — except joining the 9 in 10 sites giving AI nothing to work with.

FAQ

What is llms.txt?

A short text file at yoursite.com/llms.txt, written in plain Markdown, that summarizes a site for AI systems. Robots.txt decides who gets in. llms.txt is different — a map, not a gate, meant to help AI understand a site's structure and key pages without crawling everything first.

What does a real llms.txt file example look like?

Short and plain: a heading naming the site, a one-paragraph summary, then a couple of sections linking to the most important pages, each with a one-line description. There's no fixed schema — a clear title, an honest summary, and organized links is the pattern nearly every real example follows, aoaudit.com's included.

How do I check if my website has an llms.txt file?

Type yoursite.com/llms.txt into a browser. A 404 means no file exists. A free AO Audit scan checks for it too, automatically, alongside schema markup and AI-crawler access, so it's not a one-file-at-a-time job.

How do I create an llms.txt file?

Open any text editor. Write a heading with your business name, a short summary of what you do, and link out to your key pages with one-line descriptions. Save it as llms.txt, upload it to your site's root folder. No generator tool needed — most small businesses can write one in about fifteen minutes.
