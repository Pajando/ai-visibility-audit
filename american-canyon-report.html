The State of AI Visibility in American Canyon: we examined 28 local businesses

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit, Napa CA · Scans run July 28, 2026 · Aggregates only — no individual scores published

First, the problem in plain words: your customers have started asking AI — ChatGPT, Siri, Google's AI results — "who should I go to?" And AI doesn't answer with a page of ten blue links the way old Google did. It answers with one or two names. Most local businesses aren't in those answers — not because they're worse, but because their websites never told the machines what machines need to hear. That's invisibility by configuration, not by choice — and every gap this report found is fixable.

The one-sentence finding: of 28 locally-owned American Canyon businesses we examined, 25% have no website at all, and while nearly every site that does exist opened the door to our automated reader (20 of 21 — far better than Napa), most are readable but unverifiable: 65% lack the structured data machines use to confirm a business is real, and 60% publish no prices a machine could quote.

Method, briefly: 28 currently-operating, locally-owned American Canyon businesses — 21 with working websites across restaurants, salons, trades, auto, dental, retail, fitness, veterinary and professional offices (all scanned July 28, 2026 with our own automated checker; 20 were reachable, 1 refused the connection) plus 7 established, well-reviewed businesses for which repeated searching found no owned website at all. And one roster note we're proud of: the target was 30 websites and we published 21, because getting to 30 would have meant counting national chains, closed restaurants, or expired domains as living local businesses — and we don't. We publish aggregates only. Honest limit: a block against our reader proves a site rejects automated visitors like ours; individual AI crawlers may be treated differently by security systems — we report what we measured, not more.

Finding 1 — in a town of chains, the independents fight for the shelf

American Canyon isn't Napa. It's a city of about 21,000 on the Highway 29 corridor whose commercial spine is a handful of strip-mall plazas anchored by national chains — and the chains have corporate teams quietly doing this exact work for them. The locally-owned layer is real and it's the soul of the place: a third-generation nursery that's been family-run since 1954 (Mid City Nursery), and a strong Filipino- and Latino-owned business community — Le Paris Artisan & Gourmet Cafe, Raíces, Dos Botellas, Vargas Mexican Market. But that layer is thinner than Napa's, which makes each independent's digital shelf space matter more. A quarter of the local businesses we examined (7 of 28) have no website at all — established, reviewed businesses running entirely on Facebook, Instagram, or booking apps. Those platforms are fine tools, but to an AI deciding who to recommend, a profile on someone else's platform isn't a source it can independently read, verify, and quote the way it can an owned site. In a chain town, the owned, machine-readable website is the independent's best shot at being the answer.

Finding 2 — the doors are open; the ID cards are missing

Here's where American Canyon beats Napa: in Napa, 23% of the sites we scanned turned our automated reader away at the door. In American Canyon, only 1 of 21 sites was unreachable, and not a single site served blank pages to bots. The machines can walk right in. What they find inside is the problem: 65% of readable sites carry no LocalBusiness structured data — the machine-readable ID card that lets an AI confirm a business exists, where it is, and when it's open — 60% publish no visible prices, and 40% don't even have a meta description. Readable but unverifiable means an AI can look at you and still not be able to vouch for you. The fix is configuration, not a rebuild.

Finding 3 — the small-town surprise: llms.txt at double Napa's rate

The stat we didn't expect: 45% of American Canyon's readable sites (9 of 20) carry an llms.txt AI-instructions file — more than double the 22% we measured in Napa, a town with far more marketing budget per block. Whatever the cause — deliberate choice or a good default from whoever built these sites — the lesson stands: AI-readiness is not a big-city privilege, and your neighbor may already be ahead of you. One honest note on the other side of the ledger: one readable site in the sample actively blocks an AI crawler in its robots.txt. Whether that's deliberate or a template default someone never reviewed, it's the kind of setting an owner should know about — it's exactly what the free scan checks.

Finding 4 — the bright spots (named with respect)

Seven businesses carry proper LocalBusiness-type structured data: Dos Botellas, Kob's Kitchen, Ristorante Pasta D'oro, Hairworks Salon & Barber, Ramirez Auto, Canyon West Dental, and Madrigal Tax Firm. Nine carry the llms.txt AI-instructions file, including Mid City Nursery, BREWED, Mitchell's Landscaping, American Canyon ATA Martial Arts, and South Kelly Grill. Four appear on both lists — Kob's Kitchen, Ristorante Pasta D'oro, Ramirez Auto, and Canyon West Dental — a Lao-Thai kitchen that started as a food truck, an Italian restaurant, a family auto shop, and a dental office, quietly running some of the most AI-ready websites in southern Napa County. And there's something fitting about the town's oldest business: Mid City Nursery, family-run since 1954, carries an AI-instructions file that most of the modern internet still lacks. The standard is real and locally achievable — these businesses prove it. (We name only the leaders. Nobody gets called out here; every business in the sample can request its own private result, free.)

What this means if you own a business in American Canyon

Between the businesses with no website and the readable-but-unverifiable sites, most of American Canyon's independents can't currently be confirmed by the machines — and the machines are now the concierge, the phone book, and the first opinion. The fixes, in leverage order: get a real website if you have none · install LocalBusiness schema · publish your prices and real answers · add an llms.txt file · keep your profiles agreeing everywhere.
