The State of AI Visibility in Napa: we scanned 40 local businesses

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit, Napa CA · Scans run July 26, 2026, plus a targeted home/senior-care sweep run August 15, 2026 · Aggregates only — no individual scores published

The one-sentence finding: of 40 locally-owned Napa businesses we examined, 18% have no website at all, another 21% of the sites that exist turned away our automated reader entirely, and most of the rest are readable but unverifiable to machines — invisible not by choice, but by configuration. When a billion people now ask AI who to visit, that's the whole game. All of it is fixable.

Finding 1 — Napa's tasting rooms have a bouncer problem

Every downtown tasting room in our sample was unreachable to our automated reader — firewall blocks or failed connections. A human with a browser sees a beautiful site; a machine knocks and the door stays shut. That matters more here than almost anywhere: wine country's entire customer journey is a recommendation moment, and the concierge is now ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity — systems that send automated readers to verify a business before saying its name. When the reader bounces, the AI recommends from stale third-party scraps, or recommends someone else. The fix is usually configuration, not a rebuild: admit verified crawlers at the firewall, repair the certificate chain, then re-test.

Finding 2 — the tire shop is more AI-ready than the celebrated restaurants

The most AI-ready businesses in our Napa sample aren't who you'd guess. A plumber, a landscaper, two auto shops, and two home-care agencies added in the August sweep — Atlas Home Health and A Partner In Caring — carry llms.txt AI-instruction files, while restaurants with national reputations carry none. Being visible to AI isn't about size, budget, or being "techy": it's plumbing-level configuration, usually decided by whoever built your website. Which means two things if you own a Napa business: you have no excuse — and no protection. The neighbor you least expect may already be ahead of you.

FAQ

How AI-visible are Napa businesses?

In the first study of its kind (The AO Audit, July-August 2026, 40 locally-owned Napa businesses): 18% had no website at all; of those with websites, 21% turned away automated readers entirely (firewall blocks or connection failures); and of the sites machines could read, 62% lacked LocalBusiness structured data, 73% had no AI-instructions file (llms.txt), and 73% published no visible prices. What does it mean that a website blocks automated readers?

Some firewalls and misconfigured security certificates admit human browsers but reject automated visitors. AI systems send automated readers to fetch and verify pages before recommending a business — if those readers are turned away, the AI relies on stale third-party data or skips the business. The fix is usually configuration, not a rebuild: allow verified search and AI crawlers, and repair the certificate chain.
