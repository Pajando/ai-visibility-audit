Does AI marketing actually work? What the research shows

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · August 8, 2026

Three claims get repeated constantly in this space, and we checked each one against its actual source instead of the blog that repeated it. One turned out to be a 2024 prediction that didn't come true. One is real but gets quoted with a precision the data doesn't support. One — the specific dollar-and-percentage ROI claims for AI visibility work — traces back almost entirely to vendors citing their own clients, with no independent research behind it at all.

None of that means AI marketing is fake. It means most of the proof people cite for it is weaker than it sounds, and the honest version of this topic is more useful than the confident one.

Claim 1: "Google search is dying, AI is replacing it"

The number behind this is almost always Gartner's February 2024 prediction that traditional search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026. We checked whether that happened. It didn't: Google still holds 91.31% of worldwide search engine market share as of July 2026, according to StatCounter, an independent analytics firm — essentially the same dominant position it has held for years. At the same time, ChatGPT really did reach 900 million weekly active users in February 2026, straight from OpenAI's own announcement. Both are true. AI usage is growing fast without yet displacing classic search at the market-share level. "Additive, not replacing" is the accurate read, not "Google is dying."

Claim 2: "AI marketing tools save 10+ hours a week"

This exact figure shows up, worded almost identically, across a dozen low-credibility sites with no disclosed survey or methodology — a sign they're copying each other, not measuring anything. The one credible source we could trace is HubSpot's own 2026 survey of 1,500+ marketers, and even that has an internal inconsistency worth stating plainly: one part of their reporting suggests roughly two-thirds of marketers save 10 or more hours weekly, while another part of the same research reports a 6.1-hour average. Those don't fully add up. Real time savings clearly exist. The specific number attached to it should be read as a rough range, not a precise fact.

Claim 3: "Schema markup increased our AI visibility by 55%"

This is the one worth being most direct about. Every version of this claim we found — 55% in a day, 1,194% CTR increase, six-figure revenue impact — traces back to the company selling the service, describing its own client, with no disclosed sample size, no control group, and no visible case where it didn't work. That's not conflicting research. It's marketing copy formatted to look like research. The honest state of the field: nobody, including us, has independently verified data connecting specific AI-visibility fixes to specific revenue outcomes. That's not a reason to distrust the underlying work — it's a reason to distrust anyone quoting you a precise percentage for it.

That absence of proof is exactly why this site has never promised a ranking or a mention — the guarantee behind The Answer is implementation, verified against your report, or it's free. Not a result nobody can actually measure yet.

FAQ

Does AI marketing actually work?

For the parts that can be checked: yes, with real limits. AI tool usage is genuinely growing — verified directly from OpenAI's own announcement, not a blog. Time savings from AI marketing tools are real but the specific number everyone quotes isn't reliably sourced. Whether AI-visibility work specifically drives revenue has no credible independent research yet — every number circulating for that traces back to a vendor's own case study, not verified research.

Is AI marketing legit, or mostly hype?

Both things are true at once, which is why it's confusing. The underlying shift is real — AI tools are genuinely used by hundreds of millions of people weekly. The specific statistics used to sell AI marketing services are mostly not legit — self-reported vendor case studies with no disclosed methodology, or an oft-repeated prediction that didn't actually happen. Legit trend, illegitimate proof points.

Is ChatGPT actually replacing Google search?

Not yet, based on the actual numbers. A widely-cited 2024 Gartner prediction said search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026. It didn't: Google still holds about 91% of search engine market share as of mid-2026, per StatCounter, essentially its historical position. At the same time, ChatGPT reached 900 million weekly active users in February 2026, per OpenAI's own announcement. Both are true — AI usage is growing fast without yet displacing classic search at the market-share level.

Where do the '55% increase in AI visibility' case studies come from?

Almost always from the vendor selling the service, describing their own client, with no disclosed methodology, no independent verification, and no visible downside cases. That's marketing copy structured like research, not research. It's a real evidence gap across the whole industry, not just one company's — treat any specific ROI percentage for AI visibility work as unverified until proven otherwise.
