We traced the numbers back to their source

Someone's selling you AI marketing with big numbers. Does it actually work?

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · August 8, 2026

If someone is pitching you AI marketing, they're probably quoting numbers. We traced three common ones to their source. One is a Gartner prediction for 2026 that the numbers we can check don't settle. One is real, but it's about whole teams, and the exact figure changes depending on who repeats it. The third, big ROI percentages for "AI visibility" work, we only found in vendors' own case studies.

None of that means AI marketing is fake. It means a lot of the proof sounds stronger than it is. My take: the honest version is more useful to you than the confident one.

Claim 1: "Google search is dying, AI is replacing it"

The number usually comes from Gartner's February 19, 2024 press release: "Gartner Predicts Search Engine Volume Will Drop 25% by 2026" (Gartner). Did it happen? We can't say yet. 2026 isn't over, and Gartner talked about volume, which is a different measure from market share. What we can check: Google had 89.94% of worldwide search engine market share in September 2026, per StatCounter (checked October 23, 2026). That's share among search engines, not total searches, so it can't prove or disprove Gartner. Meanwhile OpenAI said ChatGPT passed 900 million weekly active users in February 2026 (OpenAI). My take: AI use is growing fast, and Google still gets about 9 in 10 search engine visits by StatCounter's count. "Google is dying" is not what these numbers show.

Claim 2: "AI marketing tools save 10+ hours a week"

The one solid source we found is HubSpot's 2026 State of Marketing report, from a survey of over 1,500 marketers (HubSpot). It says "About one-third say AI saves their team 10 to 14 hours per week, and another third say it saves them over 15 hours," and "Just under a third" save one to nine hours. Two things to notice. These are hours saved by a whole team, not one person. And it's what marketers said, not a timed test. Other sites quote different HubSpot numbers, like a 6.1-hour weekly average, that we could not find in this report. My take: time savings are real, but treat any exact number as a rough guess.

Claim 3: "Our fix boosted AI visibility by X%"

This is the one to be most careful with. Every big percentage we found for "AI visibility" results came from the company selling the service, describing its own client. None that we found showed a sample size, a control group, or a case where it didn't work. That's marketing written to look like research. My take: I haven't seen independent data that ties a specific AI-visibility fix to a specific revenue result, and that includes our own. That's not a reason to skip the basic fixes. It's a reason to walk away from anyone quoting you an exact percentage.

FAQ

Does AI marketing actually work?

Parts of it, with real limits. AI use is growing: OpenAI said ChatGPT passed 900 million weekly active users in February 2026. Time savings are real, but the exact numbers people quote vary a lot. As for whether AI-visibility work drives revenue, we haven't found independent research on it yet. The numbers we found came from vendors' own case studies.

Is AI marketing legit, or mostly hype?

Both. The shift is real: OpenAI said ChatGPT alone passed 900 million weekly users. A lot of the stats used to sell AI marketing are weak: vendor case studies with no method shown, or predictions that haven't been checked yet. My take: real trend, shaky proof.

Is ChatGPT actually replacing Google search?

Not by the numbers we can check. Gartner predicted in February 2024 that search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026. That year isn't over, and market share doesn't measure volume. What StatCounter shows: Google had 89.94% of worldwide search engine market share in September 2026 (checked October 23, 2026). OpenAI said ChatGPT passed 900 million weekly active users in February 2026. My take: AI use is growing fast, and Google is still the main search engine by a wide margin.

Where do the big "AI visibility increase" case studies come from?

Every one we found came from the vendor selling the service, describing its own client. No method shown, no outside check, no cases where it failed. That's marketing shaped like research. My take: treat any exact ROI percentage for AI visibility work as unproven until someone shows the method.
