The State of AI Visibility in St. Helena: we examined 35 local businesses

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit, Napa CA · Scans run July 28, 2026 · Aggregates only — no individual scores published

First, the problem in plain words: the people planning a St. Helena visit — and the neighbors looking for a plumber, a dentist, a florist — have started asking AI: "what's the best tasting room in St. Helena?" "Where should we eat?" "Who can fix this today?" And AI doesn't answer with a page of ten blue links the way old Google did. It answers with one or two names. Most local businesses aren't in those answers — not because they're worse, but because their websites never told the machines what machines need to hear. That's invisibility by configuration, not by choice — and every gap this report found is fixable.

The one-sentence finding: in the prettiest town in the valley, the prettiest websites are the ones machines can't read — of 29 locally-owned St. Helena websites we scanned, 7 couldn't be read by our automated reader at all (24%), and of the 22 that could, 32% served it almost no text — beautiful, image-and-JavaScript-heavy pages that look stunning to a human and nearly blank to a machine. The highest near-blank rate of the three towns we've examined, and none of it a choice anyone made on purpose.

Method, briefly: 35 currently-operating, locally-owned St. Helena businesses — 29 with real websites across wineries, restaurants, barbershops, trades, auto, dental, retail, fitness, veterinary and professional offices (all scanned July 28, 2026 with our own automated checker; 22 were readable, 7 rejected the connection with certificate failures or firewall blocks) plus 6 established, well-reviewed businesses for which repeated searching found no owned website at all. Two roster notes for transparency: the target was 30 websites and we published 29, because the 30th would have meant counting a broken domain, an unconfirmable ownership, or an unverifiable operating status as a living local business — and we don't. And a few St.-Helena-founded operations that have since grown to a handful of locations (Gott's Roadside, Model Bakery, AERENA Galleries) are included openly: founded or rooted in town, still independently owned, not chains. We publish aggregates only. Honest limit: a block against our reader proves a site rejects automated visitors like ours; individual AI crawlers may be treated differently by security systems — we report what we measured, not more.

Finding 1 — the postcard problem: beautiful to you, blank to the machines

St. Helena is the postcard of Napa Valley — the town of V. Sattui's picnic grounds, of Michelin-starred PRESS, of a Main Street where the grocery store (Sunshine Foods) has been in the same family for four generations, and where a Latina-owned caffe (Valley Caffe, opened 2024 by longtime employee-turned-owner Jessica Mendez) rose at the very address where a beloved predecessor closed. The websites match the town: gorgeous. And that's exactly the problem we measured. Wine-country sites are design-first — full-screen photography, elegant JavaScript-rendered menus and booking widgets — and when our automated reader visited, 7 of the 22 readable sites (32%) handed it almost nothing: under 1,500 characters of actual text, and in three cases under 200. For comparison, in Napa we measured 1 near-blank site of 23 readable, and in American Canyon zero of 20. On top of that, 7 of St. Helena's 29 sites couldn't be read at all — expired-feeling certificate errors and firewalls that turn away automated visitors outright. Add it up and 14 of the 29 websites — roughly half — give machines little or nothing to work with. Nobody chose this. It's the default behavior of design-first templates and security settings someone else configured. A human sees beauty; the AI deciding who to recommend sees a blank page — invisibility by a design choice nobody made on purpose. The fix is configuration, not a redesign: the photos stay, the text just has to exist where machines can read it.

Finding 2 — the triple 77: readable isn't the same as verifiable

Even among the sites machines can read, the same number came up three times: 77% carry no LocalBusiness structured data (the machine-readable ID card that lets an AI confirm a business exists, where it is, and when it's open), 77% publish no visible prices a machine could quote, and 77% have no llms.txt AI-instructions file — each 17 of 22. Readable but unverifiable means an AI can look at you and still not be able to vouch for you. One more honest note: one readable site in the sample actively blocks six named AI crawlers in its robots.txt. Whether that's deliberate or a security default someone never reviewed, it's exactly the kind of setting an owner should get to decide on purpose — and exactly what the free scan checks.

This is our third town, and each one fails differently — which is itself the lesson: there is no one wine-country problem, there's only your configuration.

Napa's problem was the bouncer at the door. American Canyon's was having no building at all. St. Helena's is subtler and, for a town whose whole economy runs on being recommended, maybe costlier: the buildings are magnificent and the machines can't see inside.

Finding 4 — the bright spots (named with respect)

Five businesses carry proper LocalBusiness-type structured data: Gott's Roadside, Hail Mary Barber + Apothecary, Fulton Body Shop, Daisy Clothing Boutique, and Sunshine Foods. Five carry an llms.txt AI-instructions file: Gott's Roadside, Fulton Body Shop, Up Valley Dentist, Sunshine Foods, and St. Helena Florist. Three appear on both lists — Gott's Roadside, Fulton Body Shop, and Sunshine Foods — a burger stand born in St. Helena in 1999 on the site of a 1949 roadside classic, a family collision shop with four decades on Fulton Lane, and the town's own grocery store. And there's something fitting about that last one: Sunshine Foods, four generations in the same family since 1975, runs the most machine-readable website in our entire sample — structured data, visible prices, an AI-instructions file, and more readable text than any other site we scanned. The standard is real and locally achievable — the town grocer proves it. (We name only the leaders. Nobody gets called out here; every business in the sample can request its own private result, free.)

What this means if you own a business in St. Helena

Between the sites machines can't reach, the sites that serve them almost nothing, and the readable-but-unverifiable rest, most of St. Helena's independents can't currently be read, verified, and quoted by the machines — and the machines are now the concierge, the phone book, and the first opinion for every visitor planning a trip up Highway 29. The fixes, in leverage order: let verified crawlers through your firewall and fix the certificate · make your text exist as text, not only as images and scripts · install LocalBusiness schema · publish your prices and real answers · add an llms.txt file · keep your profiles agreeing everywhere. Questions people ask

How AI-visible are St. Helena businesses?

In the first study of its kind for the town (The AO Audit, July 2026, 35 locally-owned St. Helena businesses): 17% had no website at all; of the 29 sites that exist, 7 could not be read by our automated checker at all (24%); and of the 22 readable sites, 32% served our reader almost no text — the highest near-blank rate of the three towns we've examined — while 77% lacked LocalBusiness structured data, 77% published no visible prices, and 77% had no AI-instructions file (llms.txt). St. Helena has some of the most beautiful websites in Napa County, and the highest share machines can barely read.

Why does the report cover 29 websites instead of 30?

Because we refused to pad the list. Getting to 30 would have meant counting a business whose own marketed domain redirects to an unrelated website, a lodging property we couldn't confirm as locally owned, and a restaurant whose current operating status we couldn't verify. 29 verified, locally-owned, currently-operating businesses with real websites is what honest checking produced — plus 6 established local businesses with no website at all, for a universe of 35 examined.

Which St. Helena businesses did well in the report?

Five businesses carry proper LocalBusiness-type structured data: Gott's Roadside, Hail Mary Barber + Apothecary, Fulton Body Shop, Daisy Clothing Boutique, and Sunshine Foods. Five carry an llms.txt AI-instructions file: Gott's Roadside, Fulton Body Shop, Up Valley Dentist, Sunshine Foods, and St. Helena Florist. Three appear on both lists — Gott's Roadside, Fulton Body Shop, and Sunshine Foods — and the most machine-readable site in the whole sample belongs to Sunshine Foods, the family grocery that has fed the town for four generations.
