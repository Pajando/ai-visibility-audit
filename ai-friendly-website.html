Three things, not a vibe

Customers ask AI who to call. Can it even read your website?

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · August 6, 2026

A customer asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google for a bakery in Napa. If those tools can't reach your site, your site can't be part of the answer. If they can reach it but can't find your hours or prices, they have less to go on. Three things you can check: 1) the bots that fetch pages for AI answers are allowed in, 2) your page says plainly what you are, where you are and what you offer, 3) your real prices, hours and answers are written as text. My take: most owners can check all three without a developer.

First, what Google says. Its guide to AI features says: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features." (Google Search Central). So schema is not a ticket into Google's AI answers. My take: it still helps, because it states your facts in a form any machine can read without guessing. Just pick the right type.

Structured data: pick the type that tells the truth

Structured data is a block of code, usually JSON-LD, sitting in a page's head, stating your business facts in a format machines read directly instead of inferring from paragraphs of copy. A minimal example, for a business with a real storefront:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "Example Bakery",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "123 Main St",
    "addressLocality": "Napa",
    "addressRegion": "CA"
  },
  "telephone": "+1-707-555-0100",
  "openingHours": "Mo-Sa 07:00-16:00"
}

Schema.org defines LocalBusiness as "A particular physical business or branch of an organization" (schema.org). My take: if customers walk in, or you drive to them in a set area, it fits. If you work fully online with no place customers visit, Organization is the more honest label. This site is an example: The AO Audit has no storefront, so its homepage schema uses Organization, not LocalBusiness.

The other half: can AI crawlers even reach you?

Open yoursite.com/robots.txt in a browser. If it's blank or missing, crawlers are allowed by default. If it lists rules, look for the bots that fetch pages for AI answers: OAI-SearchBot (ChatGPT search), PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot and Claude-User. OpenAI says: "To make a website eligible for inclusion, allow OAI-Searchbot to crawl the site" (OpenAI Help). Perplexity says PerplexityBot "is designed to surface and link websites in search results" (Perplexity docs). Anthropic says blocking Claude-SearchBot may reduce your visibility in its search results (Anthropic). GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended are different: they control AI training. Google says Google-Extended "does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search" (Google).

Your robots.txt is not the only gate. On July 1, 2025, Cloudflare, a common security service, announced it would block AI crawlers by default and ask every new domain at sign-up whether to allow them (Cloudflare). In our own scan of 257 local businesses (July 26 to August 15, 2026), 37 of the 205 sites with a website blocked our automated reader. We didn't check what caused each block. Here is part of this site's robots.txt. The full file names 15 bots, and all of them are allowed:

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot

What none of this does: make any AI tool name you. OpenAI says it plainly: "Placement is not guaranteed." (OpenAI Help). What these checks do is remove the reasons a tool can't reach or read your site.

FAQ

How do you make a website AI-friendly?

Check three things. 1) Your robots.txt and your security settings let in the bots that fetch pages for AI answers, like OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot. 2) Your page says plainly what you are, where you are and what you offer. Structured data (schema) can say it in code too, but Google says you don't need special markup to appear in its AI features. 3) Your real prices, hours and answers are written as text, not hidden in images. My take: most owners can check all three without a developer.

What is structured data for a local business?
