Customers ask AI and never hear your name? Here's what GEO is, and what the research found

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · July 30, 2026

The short answer: GEO means writing your content so AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity are more likely to use it in their answers. The name comes from a research paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", accepted to KDD 2024, by a team that includes Princeton researchers. They built a benchmark of 10,000 queries and tested nine ways to rewrite a web page. The best ones (adding quotes, adding statistics, citing sources) raised a page's visibility in the AI answer by up to 40% in their tests. Keyword stuffing gave "little to no improvement."

My take: a lot of GEO advice online never links the paper that coined the word. The paper is worth reading because it doesn't guess. It built a benchmark called GEO-bench: 10,000 queries, mostly real search queries from public datasets, plus some the authors had GPT-4 write. Most tests ran on an AI answer engine the researchers built with GPT-3.5, with a smaller check on Perplexity. Here's what it found.

What the research measured

StrategyWhat it meansEffect on visibility
Cite sourcesBacking claims with named, trusted references inside your textRaised visibility. One of the three best methods.
Add quotationsIncluding direct quotes from real people or sourcesThe biggest gain in the main test. The paper says it worked best in "People & Society," "Explanation" and "History" questions.
Add statisticsIncluding real, specific numbersOne of the strongest gains. It helped most in "Law & Government" and opinion questions.
Keyword stuffingRepeating target words over and over"Little to no improvement," in the paper's words
Clearer, smoother writingMaking the text easier to read (the paper's "Fluency Optimization" and "Easy-to-Understand" methods)A "significant visibility boost of 15-30%," per the paper. How you write counts too.

Source: Aggarwal, Murahari, Rajpurohit, Kalyan, Narasimhan & Deshpande, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (KDD 2024). Results changed by topic. These are controlled tests, not live results for any one business.

What this means for a local business

My take: look at what won. Real sources, real numbers, real quotes, clear writing. For a local business, that means real prices instead of "call for a quote," plain facts about what you do and where, and words from real customers. One warning: the paper did not test schema markup or local business sites. If someone tells you this study "proved" schema works, it didn't. It tested rewritten text on general questions. FAQ

What is GEO in marketing?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) means shaping your content so AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode are more likely to use it when they answer a question. The term comes from a research paper accepted to KDD 2024, by a team that includes Princeton researchers. It did not come from a marketing agency.

Does GEO actually work? What does the research say?

In the paper's tests, yes, with limits. The researchers built a 10,000-query benchmark. Adding quotes, statistics and cited sources raised a page's visibility in AI answers by up to 40%. Clearer, smoother writing also helped, by 15-30%. Keyword stuffing gave "little to no improvement." These were controlled tests, mostly on an answer engine the researchers built, so treat them as a direction, not a promise.

What's the difference between GEO and AEO?

My take: very little in practice. See the full AEO vs GEO vs SEO comparison for the plain-English breakdown. GEO comes from academic research. AEO is the name the SEO industry uses for much the same shift.

Do I need to hire a GEO agency?

Not necessarily. The methods that worked in the research (citing real sources, adding real numbers, quoting real people) are things you can do yourself. My take: cover the basics too.
