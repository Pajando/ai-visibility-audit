What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)?

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · July 30, 2026

The short answer: GEO is the practice of structuring content so generative AI engines — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity — cite it in their answers. The term comes from a real 2024 Princeton/Georgia Tech study that tested 10,000 real queries and measured, precisely, what improves a source's odds of being cited: citing sources, adding statistics, and adding direct quotations lifted visibility by up to 40%. Keyword stuffing did not.

Most articles that use the word "GEO" never open the paper that coined it. That's a shame, because the paper is unusually concrete — it doesn't guess at what works, it built a 10,000-query benchmark called GEO-bench and measured nine different content strategies against it. Here's what it actually found.

What the research measured

StrategyWhat it meansEffect on visibility
Cite sourcesBacking claims with named, authoritative referencesMeasurably improved citation odds
Add quotationsIncluding direct quotes, especially in People & Society, Explanation, and History topicsAmong the strongest gains — up to 40% in the right domain
Add statisticsIncluding real, specific numbers — especially effective in Law & Government contentAmong the strongest gains
Keyword stuffingRepeating target terms unnaturallyNo measurable improvement
Stylistic paddingGeneric "authoritative-sounding" filler languageNo measurable improvement

Source: Aggarwal, Murahari, Rajpurohit, Kalyan, Narasimhan & Deshpande, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (KDD 2024). The paper found effectiveness varies by topic domain — the table above reflects where each strategy performed strongest.

Is that a checklist — or a coincidence?

Look at what actually worked: cited sources, real statistics, real quotations. That's not a secret trick. It's the same thing a careful local-business scan checks for — valid schema markup that names your business as the source, real prices and real numbers instead of vague copy, and real reviews instead of marketing filler. The research didn't discover a new industry. It measured, with numbers, why the honest version of a website already outperforms the padded one.

Honest limit: the 40% figure is a benchmark result under controlled test conditions, not a guarantee for any single business on any single day. Nobody — under any acronym — can promise a specific AI will cite you tomorrow. What the research shows is direction: real sources beat padding. That part travels.

FAQ

What is GEO in marketing?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the practice of structuring content so generative AI engines — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Mode — cite it when answering a user's question. The term comes from a 2024 Princeton/Georgia Tech research paper, not a marketing agency.

Does GEO actually work? What does the research say?

The paper that coined GEO tested 10,000 real queries and found that adding cited sources, direct quotations, and relevant statistics to content improved its visibility in AI-generated answers by up to 40%. Keyword stuffing and stylistic padding did not produce a measurable improvement.

What's the difference between GEO and AEO?

Almost nothing in practice — see the full AEO vs GEO vs SEO comparison for the plain-English breakdown. GEO comes from academic research; AEO is the SEO industry's name for the same shift.
