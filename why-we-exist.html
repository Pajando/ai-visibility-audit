Why we exist

When customers ask Google or AI who to call, big brands have help. Our neighbors have us.

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · Napa, California

Customers now ask Google and AI tools who to call. If those tools can't read your business, you may not be in the answer, and nobody tells you.

Google says its AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly active users in about a year. CEO Sundar Pichai said it at Google I/O on May 19, 2026. My take: when someone asks one of these tools who to call for a plumber or where to eat, they often get a short answer with a few names, not ten blue links. If you're not one of those names, that customer may never see you.

My take: the businesses left out usually aren't worse. They're the taquería with thirty years of regulars, the barber whose chair your father sat in, the plumber everyone in the neighborhood calls. A lot of them are just hard for software to read. Our own scans show it: of 257 local businesses we checked from July 26 to August 15, 2026, 52 had no website, and 37 more had sites that blocked our automated reader. Blocking our reader doesn't prove Google or AI can't see them, but it's a warning sign. My take: a lot of this is setup, not choice.

Here's the fair part: this is an information gap, not a talent gap. And it isn't about secret tricks. Google says "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features." The basics count: a website Google can find, open and read. My take: the businesses that get left out are mostly the ones nobody told.

Who gets told, and who doesn't

My take: big companies have agencies watching this every day. The businesses on the other end hear about it last. Family-run, one location, owner does the books on Sunday. They're also the easiest to sell something dishonest when they finally go looking. This field is full of guaranteed-#1 promises and numbers nobody can trace to a source. We checked those numbers ourselves.

That's the gap we exist in. Not because AI visibility is exotic work. It isn't. It's because the people who most need it are the least likely to be handed an honest version of it.

What we won't do

We won't tell you we can get you to the top of ChatGPT. OpenAI's own help page says "Placement is not guaranteed." Anyone selling that is selling weather. We won't quote you a percentage lift we can't trace to real research. My take: we haven't found independent revenue data for this work yet, and we said so in public instead of borrowing someone else's number. We won't count anything you tell us about yourself toward your score. Only what a machine can check on its own counts. And the scan doesn't obligate you to anything.

The honest limit: we're a small company working in one valley, and we don't have years of outcome data to show you. What we have is a tool that shows its work, our own published scans of 257 local businesses, and a habit of publishing our own mistakes. Judge us on that.

Where this goes

My take: making a business easy for Google and AI tools to read is not expensive or complicated. It's mostly free, often a weekend of work, and mostly nobody's job. That's why it doesn't get done. Our job is closing the gap between "this is fixable" and "someone fixed it," in a way you can check at every step.

Common questions

What is The AO Audit and who runs it?

The AO Audit is a Napa, California company. Its free scan runs 21 automated checks on your website to see how easily Google and AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity can read it, then shows what to fix first. It doesn't ask those AI tools about you, and the score doesn't predict what they'll say. It was founded by Alejandro Ojeda, whose name and face are on it, and who answers the email himself. AO stands for Answer Optimization. The wider industry also calls this AEO or GEO.

Why does The AO Audit exist?

Because customers now ask Google and AI tools who to call. Google says its AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly active users in about a year. My take: the local businesses that earned their reputation over decades are often the hardest for those tools to read. Not because they're worse, but because nobody told them what software needs. Big brands have agencies for this. Small local businesses mostly have nobody, and they're the easiest to sell something dishonest when they go looking.

Why is the scan free?

Because a business owner shouldn't have to pay to find out whether there's a problem. The scan runs on the real website, produces a real score with every check shown, and the full report costs nothing. Paid options exist, from a $39 do-it-yourself playbook to done-for-you fixes and deeper paid audits, but the free scan's full report is never locked behind a payment.

Is The AO Audit legitimate, or another AI marketing pitch?

Judge a company by what it does that would make no sense if it were lying. This one published its own failing grade twice (69, then 71). It disclosed a bug where its own scanner briefly gave a false perfect score. It scanned 257 businesses across seven towns and gave the results away. It publishes Spanish versions of its guides. And it refuses to guarantee rankings or AI mentions, because nobody controls those. Every one of those is checkable on this site.
