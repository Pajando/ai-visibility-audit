We audited our own pages

Customers ask Siri for a business like yours. Here's what Siri reads, and the sentence we got wrong.

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · August 17, 2026

How does Siri choose businesses? Think of two doors. For place lookups, like the nearest taqueria, Apple says the place card you claim shows up "across Apple Maps, Messages, Wallet, Siri, and other apps." That's the free Siri business listing. For broader questions, Apple says the new Siri can "go out to the web to get up-to-date information." That door reads the open web, not just your listing. On July 17 we banned ourselves from saying Apple was the only source. On August 17 we found that banned sentence still on our own website.

This is the work the industry calls AEO or GEO. We call it Answer Optimization (AO). My take: a Siri business listing is the kind of thing people explain with confidence and rarely check, which is why we keep publishing our own mistakes. We got it wrong once, said so, and then didn't finish the fix.

What we banned, and what we found a month later

Back on July 17 we were reading Apple's documentation to double-check a line in our scanner. Our copy said Apple was the only source Siri checks for local businesses. Apple's own words did not support that. We wrote a rule for ourselves that day: never claim Apple is the only source. Then we went through the scanner, the playbook, the report generator and the email template and pulled the sentence out.

On August 17 we went back and counted every place our site talks about Siri. Twenty-one mentions across fourteen files. Thirteen of those files were clean. One was not.

The scanner asks people a question about claiming Bing Places and Apple Business. The helper text under the question had the corrected Siri line. The fix text, the part we show you after you answer, was the old one. Here are both, exactly as they were live on August 17:

Helper text:
"Bing Places feeds Microsoft Copilot and Alexa; Apple Business is what
Siri checks for local businesses (Siri's harder questions hand off to
ChatGPT ...)"

Fix text:
"... and business.apple.com (the only data source Siri and Apple
Intelligence check for local businesses)."

Same question, two sentences, opposite claims. One says Siri has two brains. The other says Apple is the only one. Our July cleanup caught the first sentence and walked right past the second, because we searched for one phrase and this one was worded differently.

One warning: the helper text's other lines (Bing feeding Copilot and Alexa, Siri handing harder questions to ChatGPT) were our own wording at the time, not Apple's or Microsoft's. This article doesn't verify them, so don't treat them as fact.

We publish a tool that checks other people's websites. Our own copy fooled us anyway. That's the honest version, and it's the reason this article exists.

How does Siri choose businesses, in Apple's words

Apple has been clear about this, if you read the announcement instead of the blog posts about it. When Apple introduced Business Connect on January 11, 2023, it called it "a free tool that allows businesses of all sizes to claim their location place cards" appearing "across Apple Maps, Messages, Wallet, Siri, and other apps."

Read that carefully. Siri is on the list of places your claimed listing shows up. Apple never said it was the only thing Siri consults. That inference was ours, and it was wrong.

The second door matters too. Apple's June 8, 2026 announcement says the new Siri can "go out to the web to get up-to-date information using broad world knowledge." So for broader questions, what's on the web counts: your website, your other listings, and whatever else is out there about you. Apple doesn't say how much it leans on your Apple listing for those answers, so we won't guess.

Both doors changed in 2026, before we caught it

Here's what makes this worth a whole article instead of a footnote: the first door has a new name, and Apple changed it months before our audit.

Apple's newsroom post introducing Apple Business, dated March 24, 2026, says plainly that "Apple Business Essentials, Apple Business Manager, and Apple Business Connect will no longer be available once Apple Business launches." Apple said it would launch April 14, 2026, "as a free service." The same post says business info appears across Apple Maps, Mail, Wallet, Siri, Safari, Spotlight and more. It also said ads in Apple Maps were coming, and that businesses "will need to first claim their location on Maps."

So the claiming step survives the rename. Good. But if you wrote "Apple Business Connect" into a client checklist last year, that checklist is out of date now.

The second door changed too. In a January 12, 2026 joint statement, Apple and Google said "the next generation of Apple Foundation Models will be based on Google's Gemini models and cloud technology." Apple's own June 8, 2026 post says its new models were "custom-built in collaboration with Google and its Gemini models." My take: the point doesn't depend on which company wins. The model behind door two keeps changing, and you don't control any of them.

Bet on the door, not the brain.

A second thing we had slightly wrong

While auditing, we checked our own shorthand that ChatGPT is "fed by Bing". We had been using it as a reason Bing Places matters.

OpenAI's crawler documentation, read again on October 23, 2026, lists four crawlers: GPTBot for training, ChatGPT-User for pages a user's question causes it to visit, OAI-AdsBot for ad safety, and OAI-SearchBot, which OpenAI says is "used to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT's search features." The word Bing does not appear anywhere in that document.

Outside studies have measured overlap between ChatGPT's citations and Bing. One, from Seer Interactive (February 2025, 500+ citations), found 87% or more of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top 20 results. That's one study, and it may be out of date. OpenAI's own help page says only that "ChatGPT search sometimes partners with other search providers," and links privacy policies for Microsoft and Shopify. It also says "Placement is not guaranteed." The version we can defend: OpenAI says to "allow OAI-Searchbot to crawl the site," so make sure your robots.txt doesn't block it. My take: being on Bing is a sensible extra, not the whole mechanism.

What we changed today, in the same push that published this page: the scanner's fix text no longer calls Apple the only data source Siri checks, and no longer states the Bing connection as fact. If you run a scan now you get the corrected wording. The article and the fix shipped together, which is the only version of this story we would want to read.

If you own a business, here's the short version. Claim your location with Apple Business (Apple calls it "a free service"). Claim your Bing listing at bing.com/forbusiness. Make sure your website is readable by machines. My take: those three take an afternoon, and everything else is built on top of them.

FAQ

How does Siri choose businesses?

Two systems do two jobs. For place lookups like nearby coffee or "open now," Apple says your claimed place card shows up across Maps, Siri and other apps, which is why claiming it matters. For broader questions, Apple says the new Siri can "go out to the web" for up-to-date information, so your website and other listings count too. Apple's 2023 announcement called Business Connect "a free tool" for businesses of all sizes. It's now part of Apple Business.

Is a Siri business listing free?

Yes. Apple says Apple Business is "available as a free service," and Apple said it would replace Business Connect starting April 14, 2026. Apple also announced paid ads in Maps, but the regular listing is something you claim, not buy. Apple's own note says businesses "will need to first claim their location on Maps."

Is Apple the only source Siri checks for local businesses?

No, and we published that error ourselves before catching it. Apple says your claimed place card shows up in Siri, but Apple also says the new Siri can go out to the web for broader questions. My take: anyone telling you one profile covers every Siri answer is oversimplifying. Two systems, two doors.

Does ChatGPT read Bing to answer questions?

OpenAI's crawler documentation doesn't mention Bing. It lists four crawlers, including OAI-SearchBot, "used to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT's search features." OpenAI's help page says ChatGPT search "sometimes partners with other search providers" and links Microsoft's privacy policy. One outside study (Seer Interactive, February 2025) found heavy overlap with Bing results, but that's measurement by others. The practical step is the one OpenAI names: let OAI-SearchBot crawl your site.
