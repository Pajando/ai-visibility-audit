What is a Google CID number, and why should you care?

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · August 11, 2026

What a CID actually is

It's a long ID number tied to one exact place on Google Maps. Your business name can be shared by ten other places in the country. Your CID can't. It points to exactly one listing, with exactly one review history.

That matters more than it sounds, because of what it's used for: showing exactly which listing is which.

How to find your CID number

Google doesn't show it in the Business Profile dashboard you log into to manage your listing. You have to pull it from Google Maps directly.

StepWhat to do
1Find your business on Google Maps and open the listing
2In the address bar, replace https:// with view-source: and press enter (or right-click the page and choose "View Page Source")
3Press Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F) and search the page for ludocid=
4The string of digits right after ludocid=, up to the next & or #, is your CID

There's a second method: your Maps URL often contains two strings starting with 0x, joined by a colon. The second one, converted from hexadecimal to decimal, is your CID. It's fiddlier, so try the page-source method first. My take: both are workarounds, and Maps changes often. If ludocid= isn't in the source, try this one.

The real reason this matters: a second listing can show up without you asking

A CID matters because of one real problem: a second, separate listing can show up for a business that already has one. My take: it happens most after a move, a name change, or when a service-area business gets mapped in different ways. Nobody at the business asked for the second listing. It just appears.

When that happens, your reviews split. A customer who reviewed you two years ago is attached to one listing; a customer who reviewed you last month might be attached to the other. Neither profile shows your full history, and neither looks as trustworthy as the real, combined total would.

The CID helps you fix it. When you ask Google support for help, it points to exactly which listing you mean, so nobody has to guess from your business name.

Worth doing right now, free: search your own business name on Google Maps. If you see more than one pin for the same business, that's a duplicate. Every review sitting on the wrong one is a review your real listing doesn't show.

What a CID check doesn't tell you

Being honest about the limits: finding your CID and confirming you don't have a duplicate listing is one real, specific check. It says nothing about whether your website has structured data, whether AI crawlers can actually reach your site, or whether AI tools mention you when a customer asks.
