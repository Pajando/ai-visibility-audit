Search Console Wants a Star Rating You Don't Have? Don't Fake One

By Alejandro Ojeda · Be the Answer · The sequel to fixing our own schema error, and the more important half.

File this under generative engine optimization (GEO) ethics, or as we call it, Answer Optimization (AO). My take: lying to the machines is a fast way to lose their trust.

Yesterday we published the schema error Google found on our own site and fixed it. But fixing the red error revealed something more interesting underneath: a set of yellow "warnings," and one of them came with a very tempting quick fix. We didn't take it. Here's why that refusal matters more than the fix.

What did Google offer us?

Once the real error cleared, Google Search Console still listed a few optional warnings on our product. The headline one: Missing field "aggregateRating." In plain terms: Google lists a rating as a recommended field, not a required one. Add one and the warning goes away. Clearing it is one short block of code. Every incentive pointed at adding it.

There was exactly one problem: our playbook has no reviews yet. To show a rating, we'd have to invent one. A number with nothing real behind it, dressed up as customer feedback.

So why not just add one?

Because a made-up rating isn't a shortcut. It's a landmine. Google's structured data rules say it plainly: "Don't mark up irrelevant or misleading content, such as fake reviews or content unrelated to the focus of a page." Break that rule and Google can give the page a structured data manual action, which means the page "loses eligibility for appearance as a rich result." Google adds that it "doesn't affect how the page ranks." So the cost is the rich result, plus the trust of anyone who checks. My take: the "easy win" is how a business quietly burns the trust it spent years building. We tell other owners not to do this. We're not going to do it in our own code.

The tell to watch for in anyone you hire

If a tool or an agency ever tells you to add a rating, a review, or a "trust badge" you didn't earn to clear a schema warning, end the conversation right there. They're optimizing your warnings, not your business, and they're handing you the risk. The honest move with an empty rating field is to leave it empty until real reviews fill it. Ours will fill the day our first real buyers leave feedback, with their true numbers. Not a second sooner.

What "clean schema" actually means

It doesn't mean zero yellow. It means valid and true. Our product schema is valid, its every field is something a customer can verify, and the warnings that remain are honest ones: no reviews yet (so no rating), and a digital download (so no shipping to describe). "All green" is easy if you're willing to lie. We'd rather show you a couple of honest yellows that you can check yourself.
