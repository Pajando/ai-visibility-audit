Not a trick, a mechanism

How do you get ChatGPT to recommend your business?

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · August 2, 2026

ChatGPT pulls from three checkable sources, not a black box. It runs a live web search that traces overwhelmingly back to Bing's index — about 87% of citations, per a February 2025 Seer Interactive study. It reads whatever structured data states plainly what you do, where you are, your hours, and your prices. And it weighs how consistent your Google Business Profile, your website, and everywhere else you're listed actually agree with each other. There's no separate trick here — it's the same machine-readability work that lets any AI engine find, verify, and recommend you.

Search "how to get ChatGPT to recommend my business" and most of what comes back is vague advice — "be authoritative," "get more reviews," "stay consistent" — with nothing showing you which of those actually moved the needle. Here's the honest version: three real inputs, what each one means, and how to check where you stand on each.

Already being recommended? Here's why — and how to keep it

If ChatGPT is already naming your business and you're not sure why, it's almost always one or more of: valid schema already on your site, a well-maintained Google Business Profile with real reviews, consistent information across listings, or genuine mentions from other sites that vouch for you. The useful next step isn't celebrating — it's finding out specifically which of those is doing the work, so a website redesign or a profile change doesn't accidentally break it. That's exactly what a scan is for: not just diagnosing what's broken, but confirming what's already working before you touch it.

Nobody can guarantee ChatGPT — or any AI engine — will recommend a specific business on a specific day. What you can control is whether it's able to find, verify, and quote you at all. Anyone selling a guarantee beyond that is selling something they don't control.

FAQ

How do you get ChatGPT to recommend your business?

How can ChatGPT recommend my business — what does it actually check?

When ChatGPT answers a local-business question, it typically runs a live search rather than relying only on training data. That search draws heavily on Bing's index, so being properly indexed there matters as much as Google. From there, it favors sources with clear structured data and consistent information across the web over vague, inconsistent, or unverifiable ones.
