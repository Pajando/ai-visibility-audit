Bad advice about Google and AI can cost you customers. Here are 4 AI visibility claims, checked at the source.

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit, Napa CA · August 24, 2026

The short version: Advice about getting found on Google and AI repeats a lot of confident claims nobody traces to a source. We did it once too. Below are 4 claims, each checked at the primary source, with a link so you can check us.

Claim: "The 87% ChatGPT and Bing number comes from SE Ranking"

False

We made this mistake ourselves, then fixed it. The number comes from a Seer Interactive study published February 6, 2025, not from SE Ranking. Seer says: "More than 87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top 20 organic results." It looked at 500+ citations from about 100 questions and calls that "a limited sample." It is one study from early 2025, so it may be outdated. It does not say ChatGPT gets its answers from Bing.

Claim: "Google uses over 200 ranking factors for local search"

Misleading

Google's own Business Profile help says: "Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity" (Google). It gives no count of local factors, and we found no Google page that does. The same page says: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." So a "200+ local factors" list is not a Google list.

Claim: "You register a business on Bing at bingplaces.com"

Outdated

Bing moved its business listings to bing.com/forbusiness. Bing's own October 3, 2025 post says "the domain will change from www.bingplaces.com to www.bing.com/forbusiness" (Bing). Use the new address. Guides that still say bingplaces.com are out of date.

Claim: "Amazon's AI shopping assistant is called Rufus"

Outdated

Amazon says: "On May 13, 2026, Rufus was renamed Alexa for Shopping" (Amazon). Content that still calls it Rufus is using the old name.

FAQ

Why does The AO Audit publish a fact-checking page?

Because advice about AI and search is full of confident claims nobody traces to a source. We caught ourselves repeating one before we fixed it. This page makes our claims, and the common ones we hear, checkable. Every verdict links to its source.

What does the rating scale mean?

1 means false. Higher numbers mean outdated or misleading, not flat wrong. Each claim says which, because one number can't carry that on its own.

Will you add more claims to this page?

Yes, as we check new ones. We only add a claim after we trace it to a primary source ourselves, and we link that source. Sounding right is not enough.
