How to list your business on Google, Bing, and Yelp — the one-day playbook

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit · July 29, 2026

The short answer: all three listings are free, and the whole job fits in about a day. Google: set up your Business Profile at google.com/business. Bing: go to bing.com/forbusiness and click "Import from Google Business Profile" — one click copies everything over and keeps it synced weekly. Yelp: claim your page at biz.yelp.com. We did exactly this for our own business on July 26–27, 2026, skipped every paid offer on the way, and paid $0. This article is the exact play, in order, including the traps. When you're done, check what the machines can read — free, 60 seconds.

Why three listings, and who's actually reading them

Here's the problem underneath all of this: your customers now ask AI — ChatGPT, Siri, Google's AI — who to choose. AI answers with one or two names. Most local businesses are invisible to it — not by choice, but by configuration. Every one of those gaps is fixable, and these three listings are the cheapest fixes that exist: each one is a structured, verified description of your business that a specific AI engine reads.

Step 1: How to set up your Google Business Profile

Start here, because Bing will copy from it (step 2) — get Google right and you get Bing nearly free.

Go to google.com/business signed into the Google account you want running your business, and create (or claim) your profile. Three decisions matter more than all the rest:

The business-type trap: "Online retail" vs. service business

Early in setup, Google asks what kind of business you are, and this is where we nearly went wrong. "Online retail" is for selling products online. If you sell services — even fully online ones — you want the service-business path: define a service area instead of a storefront, and if you work from home, hide your address. We run The AO Audit from home; our profile shows our service area, and our home address appears nowhere. Pick the wrong type here and your profile is the wrong shape for everything that follows.

The fields AI actually reads: services and description

Most owners fill in name, phone, hours — and stop. But the fields a machine leans on to decide whether you're the answer are the ones most profiles leave empty: the services list (add every real service, individually, in the words customers use) and the business description (say plainly what you do, for whom, and where — and if your business name uses initials, spell out what they stand for; a machine can't recommend what it can't parse).

Verification: the part we can't script for you

After setup, Google assigns you a verification method — commonly a video for service-area businesses — and you don't get to choose it. Ours verified instantly, no video and no postcard, and the trust signals we believe earned that are their own story: how to get verified on Google Business Profile — and why ours verified instantly. Read it before you apply; the whole lever is what exists before you ask.

Setting up for reviews

Once verified, your profile gives you a short review link to share with customers. Asking for Google reviews is allowed and worth doing systematically — here's how to ask for reviews AI can actually read. Hold that thought, though, because the same habit is prohibited on Yelp (step 3).

Step 2: How to list your business on Bing — the one-click shortcut

First, the address, because the internet is full of the dead one: Bing Places for Business lives at bing.com/forbusiness. The old bingplaces.com is dead — Bing moved the whole thing in October 2025. If a guide sends you to bingplaces.com, the rest of that guide is old too.

Now the part that makes this the easiest listing you'll ever create. On the Bing Places setup, there is a button: "Import from Google Business Profile." We clicked it. One click imported our entire Google listing — name, service area, services, description, the lot — verified it on the spot, and turned on a weekly auto-sync from Google. Total time: one click. One listing, and it covers Bing search and Bing Maps both.

Why the import is the whole strategy: it makes your Google Business Profile the single source of truth. From then on, "how do I update my Bing business listing?" answers itself — you update Google, and the weekly sync carries it to Bing. You never maintain two listings, and they can never drift apart and disagree — and disagreement between your own listings is exactly the kind of gap that makes a machine trust you less. You can edit the Bing listing directly at bing.com/forbusiness, but if sync is on, do your editing in Google and let Bing follow.

And the payoff for one minute of work: Bing feeds Microsoft Copilot, and it's the index ChatGPT's search leans on — a February 2025 study by Seer Interactive found ~87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top results. The least-glamorous listing on this page is the one wired into the chatbot everyone's actually using.

Step 3: How to set up your Yelp business page

Go to biz.yelp.com and search for your business — Yelp may already have a page for you that you've never controlled. Claim it (or add your business if it's not there). Three things from our own setup:

1. The 6-service-area cap. Yelp lets you list at most 6 service areas. That cap forced us to actually prioritize: we picked the 6 places our customers really are, instead of the everywhere-blur we'd have typed into an unlimited field. Treat the constraint as a feature — choose the 6 that matter.

2. Hide your address. If you work from home, Yelp has a toggle to hide your street address while keeping your service areas public. Use it, same as on Google.

3. Never ask for reviews on Yelp. This is the one that catches honest owners: Yelp prohibits soliciting reviews — even politely, even from genuinely happy customers — and penalizes pages for it. The review-ask habit that's correct on Google is a violation on Yelp. Ask on Google; on Yelp, just be excellent and let reviews happen.

Why bother at all? Yelp's business data is what Alexa+ draws on for local recommendations. That's a reader you can't reach any other way.

The traps: what all three platforms will try to sell you on the way

Here's what nobody tells you about "free" listings: the listing is genuinely free, but the setup flow on every one of these platforms is also a sales funnel, and it's tuned to catch you at the exact moment you're in let's-finish-this mode. Everything below appeared during our own three setups. None of it is a scam — ads are a real product — but none of it is a setup step either, and each one is dressed as one:

The promptWhat it actually isWhat we did
"$500 in free ad credit!"An ad credit that requires matching spend and a credit card on file — it's an ads subscription with a coupon on topSkipped
"Offer expires in 2 hours!"A countdown timer manufacturing urgency. The deadline is not real — the same offer is there tomorrowSkipped
"Add these categories too?"Category-dilution prompts nudging you to claim things you don't really do — which blurs exactly the signal the machines readSkipped; kept categories to what we actually do
Upgrade screens mid-setupPaid products placed between you and the finish line, styled like required stepsSkipped — there's always a "skip," "not now," or quiet link past them

The rule that got us through all three platforms unscathed: during setup, skip every paid prompt. The listings themselves are free, verified is free, and everything the AI engines read is free. If ads ever make sense for you, decide that later, on purpose, with a data case — never mid-setup with a fake clock running.

FAQ

How do you set up a Google Business Profile?

Go to google.com/business signed into your business's Google account, enter name, category, and location or service area, then fill the fields AI reads — services and description. Verification comes last: our full guide, including our instant verification.

How do you set up a Google Business Profile for an online business?

If you sell services, set up as a service-area business with your address hidden — "Online retail" is for selling products online. We run a home-based online business exactly this way.

How do I list my business on Bing Places and Bing Maps?

At bing.com/forbusiness (bingplaces.com is dead), use "Import from Google Business Profile" — one click imports, verifies, and auto-syncs weekly. One listing covers Bing search and Bing Maps.

How do I update my Bing business listing?

With the Google import on, update your Google Business Profile and the weekly sync carries it over. Direct edits at bing.com/forbusiness work too, but keep Google as the source of truth.

How do I set up a Yelp business page?

Claim or add your business at biz.yelp.com, hide your address if home-based, pick your best 6 service areas — and never solicit reviews on Yelp; it's prohibited there (Google asks are fine).

How do I set up my Google Business Profile for reviews?

Once verified, share your profile's review link systematically — here's how to ask for reviews AI can actually read. Don't do the same on Yelp.

Is it free to list a business on Google, Bing, and Yelp?

Yes — all three listings are free. The paid prompts during setup (ad credits requiring matching spend, countdown offers, upgrade screens) are optional products, not setup steps. Skip them all.
