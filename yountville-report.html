The State of AI Visibility in Yountville: 26 businesses examined

By Alejandro Ojeda · The AO Audit, Napa CA · Scans run August 9, 2026 · Aggregates only — no individual scores published

The one-sentence finding: of 26 locally-owned Yountville businesses we examined — the French Laundry among them — 19% have no website at all, another 33% of the sites that exist turned away our automated reader entirely, and most of the rest are readable but unverifiable to machines. Roughly half of Yountville's business community is invisible to the systems that now answer "where should I eat in Napa Valley?" All of it is fixable.

Method, briefly: 26 currently-operating, locally-owned Yountville businesses across restaurants, wineries, retail, inns, and trades — 21 with websites (scanned August 9, 2026 with our own automated checker) plus 5 established businesses for which repeated searching found no website at all. We publish aggregates only. Honest limit: a block against our reader proves a site rejects automated visitors like ours; individual AI crawlers may be treated differently by security systems — we report what we measured, not more. Yountville is tiny (population under 3,000), so several standard categories — auto repair, electricians, chiropractors — simply don't exist here as standalone local businesses; we didn't force substitutes.

Finding 1 — Yountville's tasting rooms have a bouncer problem, for the third town running

All four tasting rooms in our sample, plus a wine retailer and a vineyard's coffee bar, share one problem: a broken or incomplete SSL certificate chain. We caught this carefully — an early pass nearly mistook a residential ISP quirk for the real finding, until we confirmed the same certificate failure two independent ways, from two separate networks. It's real. A human browser may still connect; the automated reader that AI systems send to verify a business before naming it does not. This is the third consecutive wine-country town in our series with this exact pattern, after every sampled tasting room failed to render in St. Helena (4 of 4) and Calistoga (3 of 3). Three towns, independently, same problem. That's not bad luck — it's a category-wide gap.

Finding 2 — the French Laundry has zero structured data

Three Michelin stars. Zero JSON-LD. The French Laundry's site loads fine for a human and for our reader — but it carries no structured data of any kind, and neither do Bouchon Bistro or Ad Hoc, its siblings under the same restaurant group. A machine reading these pages can't verify what the restaurant serves, where it is, or that it's real in the way schema.org defines "real." Two doors down, Ciccio — family-owned, no Michelin stars — carries full LocalBusiness schema: address, reservations, the works. Fame doesn't correlate with AI-readiness. If anything, in this sample, it ran the other way.

Finding 3 — the bright spots (named with respect)

Two businesses carry proper LocalBusiness-type structured data: Ciccio and Bottega, both independent restaurants. Five carry an AI-instructions file (llms.txt): Ciccio, Alchemy Works, TINA Stephens, North Block Yountville, and Napa Valley Bike Tours & Rentals — a spread across restaurant, retail, hospitality, and recreation, proving this isn't a category-specific fix. The standard is real and locally achievable. (We name only the leaders. Nobody gets called out here; every business in the sample can request its own private result, free.)

What this means if you own a business in Yountville

Between the no-website businesses and the blocked sites, roughly 5 in 10 of the Yountville businesses we examined can't be read by machines at all — the highest rate in our series so far. Yountville runs on visitors asking "where should I eat, where should I taste" before they ever arrive, and increasingly that question goes to an AI, not a guidebook. FAQ

How AI-visible are Yountville businesses?

In our study (The AO Audit, August 2026, 26 locally-owned Yountville businesses): 19% had no website at all; of those with websites, 33% turned away our automated reader entirely (broken or incomplete SSL certificates, or connections actively refused); and of the sites machines could read, 86% lacked LocalBusiness structured data, 64% had no AI-instructions file (llms.txt), and 64% published no visible prices. Roughly half of the businesses we examined cannot be read by machines at all.

Fame and AI-readiness are unrelated.
